Secure Remote Access

By GG Technology Advisory • May 15, 2026 • 4 min read

Remote access is now a normal part of how work gets done, but it also creates a large portion of an organisation's exposure if not designed well. The goal is not to remove remote access; it is to enable it with controls that are strong enough to mitigate risk without creating operational friction.

Secure remote access starts with identity. Users should be required to authenticate with strong controls, and access should be tied to the device, the user and the context of the request. This is especially important when people are working from outside the corporate network.

Organisations also need to reduce the blast radius of compromise. Network segmentation, least-privilege access and well-defined role models help ensure that stolen credentials or a compromised endpoint do not become a full enterprise incident.

Modern remote access strategies often rely on approved, centrally managed access patterns rather than ad hoc software or uncontrolled VPN arrangements. That makes oversight easier and reduces the chance of shadow access paths emerging.

For many teams, the real challenge is not adding more controls but designing access in a way that still supports productivity. Secure access is not about making work harder; it is about removing unnecessary risk while preserving the speed the business needs.
