Strata Journey Part 3: Turning a Conventional Building into a Connected Building

By GG Technology Advisory • October 22, 2026 • 5 min read

Most apartment buildings were designed around the technologies available at the time they were constructed. As resident expectations evolved, however, many of these legacy systems remained largely unchanged. Over time, this stagnation leads to a familiar set of challenges for strata communities: operational inefficiencies, growing security vulnerabilities, escalating maintenance costs, and daily frustrations for residents.

When faced with ageing infrastructure, the default response in many buildings is reactive—replacing individual components like-for-like only when they fail. Rather than continuing this fragmented cycle, the committee took a fundamentally different approach by developing a holistic "Connected Building Strategy".

The objective was never to add technology for technology's sake. Instead, the strategy focused on establishing an integrated digital ecosystem where multiple building systems work together seamlessly, reducing complexity for residents while solving real, everyday operational problems.

1. Reliable Connectivity is The Essential Utility

Connectivity is the backbone of virtually every modern building technology. Whether deploying solar monitoring, access control, CCTV, smart lighting, environmental sensors, or licence plate recognition, every system relies on a resilient communications foundation.

The committee recognised early on that before any smart-building initiatives could succeed, the building needed modern digital infrastructure. At the same time, residents increasingly required faster, more reliable internet for remote work, study, and everyday life. Rather than accepting the building's legacy copper limitations, the committee engaged with major telecommunications providers to negotiate a pathway forward. After extensive discussions, a provider agreed to install new fibre-optic infrastructure directly into the building.

The outcome was transformative. Internet services that previously struggled at 20–60 Mbps were upgraded to reliable, gigabit-capable connectivity, dramatically improving performance and capacity. Building on this milestone, the committee deployed secure wired and Wi-Fi networks across common areas, plant rooms, and operational spaces.

Much like electricity or plumbing, reliable connectivity has become an essential utility. This digital backbone provided the platform upon which all subsequent security, automation, and resident experience upgrades could be integrated, allowing the building to evolve without requiring disruptive infrastructure overhauls for every new project.
