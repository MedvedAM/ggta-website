Strata Journey Part 3: Turning a Conventional Building into a Connected Building

By GG Technology Advisory • October 22, 2026 • 5 min read

Most apartment buildings were designed around the technologies available at the time they were constructed. As resident expectations evolved, however, many of these legacy systems remained largely unchanged. Over time, this stagnation leads to a familiar set of challenges for strata communities: operational inefficiencies, growing security vulnerabilities, escalating maintenance costs, and daily frustrations for residents. When faced with ageing infrastructure, the default response in many buildings is reactive—replacing individual components like-for-like only when they fail. Rather than continuing this fragmented cycle, the committee took a fundamentally different approach by developing a holistic "Connected Building Strategy". The objective was never to add technology for technology's sake. Instead, the strategy focused on establishing an integrated digital ecosystem where multiple building systems work together seamlessly, reducing complexity for residents while solving real, everyday operational problems. 1. Reliable Connectivity is The Essential Utility Connectivity is the backbone of virtually every modern building technology. Whether deploying solar monitoring, access control, CCTV, smart lighting, environmental sensors, or licence plate recognition, every system relies on a resilient communications foundation. The committee recognised early on that before any smart-building initiatives could succeed, the building needed modern digital infrastructure. At the same time, residents increasingly required faster, more reliable internet for remote work, study, and everyday life. Rather than accepting the building's legacy copper limitations, the committee engaged with major telecommunications providers to negotiate a pathway forward. After extensive discussions, a provider agreed to install new fibre-optic infrastructure directly into the building. The outcome was transformative. Internet services that previously struggled at 20–60 Mbps were upgraded to reliable, gigabit-capable connectivity, dramatically improving performance and capacity. Building on this milestone, the committee deployed secure wired and Wi-Fi networks across common areas, plant rooms, and operational spaces. Much like electricity or plumbing, reliable connectivity has become an essential utility. This digital backbone provided the platform upon which all subsequent security, automation, and resident experience upgrades could be integrated, allowing the building to evolve without requiring disruptive infrastructure overhauls for every new project. 2. From Cluttered Keyrings to Unified Digital Identity Over the years, the building had accumulated hundreds of standalone access credentials, including key fobs, garage remotes, and visitor devices. As apartment ownership and tenancies turned over, scores of credentials were lost, unreturned, or left active without clear records. This created significant operational and security headaches: Zero visibility into who actually held active building credentials. Security vulnerabilities, as legacy fobs and remotes could be easily cloned using cheap consumer technology. Ongoing financial drain from constantly purchasing and programming replacement devices. Administrative bottlenecks when auditing access rights during resident move ins and move outs. To solve this, the committee shifted from managing disconnected physical hardware to implementing a centralised resident credential management framework. Each apartment was assigned a digital unit profile within the building management system, establishing a single source of truth. Access permissions, mobile credentials, and vehicle registrations are now tied directly to the unit profile rather than individual pieces of plastic. Today, lost or unauthorised credentials can be revoked instantly, permissions are seamlessly recycled when tenants change, and residents can navigate the building using their smartphones. 3. Frictionless Access by Licence Plate Recognition, Mobile Phone Access Control & Mobile Intercoms Two of the most visible touchpoints for any resident are entering the car park and welcoming visitors at the front door. Both had historically relied on ageing hardware that caused daily friction. In the garage, traditional remote controls were replaced with Licence Plate Recognition (LPR) linked directly to each apartment's digital profile. Authorised vehicles are now recognised automatically upon approach, eliminating the need for physical remotes, reducing entry delays, and giving building management clear, auditable visibility of vehicle access activity. Similarly, the legacy intercom system which relied on fixed in-apartment handsets and ageing telephone lines was modernised into a smartphone enabled intercom platform. Residents can opt in to receive visitor calls directly on their mobile phones, wherever they are. Whether working remotely, travelling, or simply away from the apartment, residents can speak with couriers and grant secure access for parcel deliveries without missing a beat. 4. Smarter Operations by Adaptive Lighting & Proactive Sensors Beyond security and convenience, the connected backbone unlocked major improvements in how the building consumes energy and manages physical risk. Lighting across common property typically represents one of a strata scheme's largest ongoing electricity expenses, with legacy fixtures running at full brightness 24/7 regardless of occupancy. By introducing modern LED technology paired with centralised smart controls and adaptive dimming, the building now tailors illumination levels to actual operational needs. The result is a substantial reduction in electricity consumption and maintenance costs while maintaining safe, high-quality lighting throughout common areas. At the same time, the building transitioned from reactive maintenance to proactive awareness. Historically, building managers only learned of water leaks, pump failures, or abnormal plant room conditions after costly damage had already occurred or residents lodged complaints. By deploying a network of smart environmental and operational sensors connected to the building's Wi-Fi backbone, anomalies are detected early, protecting building assets and preventing minor issues from escalating into major insurance claims. 5. Reimagining Resident Communication Even the best run building struggles if residents are left in the dark. Relying on paper notices pinned to corkboards and ad hoc emails meant critical updates were frequently overlooked or out of date. By installing smart digital noticeboards in high traffic lobbies or lift areas, management can now broadcast real time building announcements, maintenance schedules, emergency alerts, and sustainability updates. Content is updated instantly from a central dashboard, eliminating printing waste and ensuring vital community information actually reaches the people who live there. The Takeaway is Strategy Before Hardware Transforming a conventional apartment building into a connected building isn't about chasing the latest gadgets. It is about treating digital infrastructure as a core asset. By laying a fibre/network/Wi-Fi foundation first, the committee turned a fragmented collection of ageing systems into a cohesive, secure, and easily managed ecosystem and enhancing daily convenience for residents while protecting the long term value of the property.
