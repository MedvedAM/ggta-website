What Are the Biggest Challenges Facing Strata Buildings and Owners Corporations Today?

Six years ago, I bought an apartment in one of Sydney's beautiful suburbs.

At the time, my career had already spanned more than 25 years in technology, digital transformation, infrastructure, vendor management and large-scale operational projects. I thought I had seen every type of challenge a modern organisation could face.

Then I discovered something entirely new: strata.

Suddenly, there was this mysterious organisation that seemed to govern every aspect of where I lived. There were quarterly levies, annual meetings, committee decisions and a long list of responsibilities that most owners rarely see or understand.

Like many owners, I initially viewed strata as something that simply existed in the background.

Six months later, I received an invitation to the AGM. Attendance was low, so I joined the call out of curiosity. By the end of that meeting, I had joined the strata committee.

One day I was simply an apartment owner. The next day I found myself helping make decisions that affected an entire community.

That's when I realised how difficult these roles really are.

All committee members are volunteers. They are expected to make decisions involving engineering, building maintenance, legal obligations, compliance, safety, energy management, contractors, budgets and long-term planning, often without having expertise in any of those areas.

Over the following five years, I became heavily involved in improving our building. Drawing on my professional experience in technology, vendor management and project delivery, I started reviewing contractor proposals, scopes of work and supplier quotations.

What I discovered was concerning.

Many Owners Corporations are spending significant amounts of money without having the information needed to properly evaluate proposals, assess long-term value or verify whether the recommended work is actually the best solution.

In many cases, the issue isn't a lack of effort. It's a lack of visibility, expertise and independent advice.

Once we started applying a more structured approach to planning, procurement and project evaluation, something changed.

Our Capital Works Fund began to grow.

Instead of constantly reacting to problems, we started investing strategically.

One of our first major initiatives was a sustainability programme. We replaced outdated lighting throughout the building with LED lighting and smart sensors. The business case was simple, with an estimated payback period of just two years.

That success created momentum.

Over time, we transformed the building with:

  • Smart access control using mobile credentials
  • Number plate recognition for vehicle access
  • Building-wide Wi-Fi and communal internet services
  • Solar generation supporting common property and residents
  • Building sensors for power, humidity, safety and security monitoring
  • Digital communication screens for residents
  • EV charging readiness infrastructure
  • Improved parcel security and delivery management
  • CCTV systems

At the same time, our common area electricity consumption dropped from approximately 6,000 kWh per month to around 1,200 kWh per month, despite supporting electric vehicle charging within the building.

The financial improvements were just as important as the technology upgrades.

Better planning and smarter investment decisions created the capacity to undertake major building improvements, including repainting works and the next phase of our sustainability journey: replacing an ageing gas hot water system with highly efficient heat-pump technology powered largely by solar energy.

What started as a personal interest became something much bigger.

People from other strata communities began asking for advice.

They were facing the same challenges:

  • Confusing contractor proposals
  • Escalating maintenance costs
  • Rising insurance premiums
  • Limited visibility of building systems
  • Difficulty prioritising capital expenditure
  • Uncertainty about sustainability investments
  • Lack of independent technical expertise

That experience ultimately led me to start helping other Owners Corporations understand what they are spending money on, what risks they should be managing, and how technology, sustainability and smart planning can improve both building performance and resident experience.

The biggest challenge facing strata today is not a lack of funding. It's a lack of information.

When committees have the right information, they make better decisions. Better decisions create savings. Savings create opportunities. And opportunities create better places for people to live.

I look forward to sharing insights, lessons learned and practical strategies from this journey in future posts.
