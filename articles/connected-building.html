Connected Building Systems: Building a Smarter, More Resilient Asset

By Grigori Medved • September 15, 2026 • 6 min read

Connected building systems are no longer a futuristic idea. They are a practical requirement for owners and operators seeking better efficiency, stronger resilience and higher asset value.

Across commercial and residential portfolios, the real challenge is not simply connecting devices. The challenge is designing an integrated technology foundation that supports occupancy, operations, maintenance and long-term sustainability without creating new complexity.

Start with outcomes, not equipment

Too many building programs begin with hardware or vendor selection before the business problem is clearly articulated. A smarter approach is to identify the operational outcomes that matter most: lower energy use, better maintenance visibility, faster incident response, improved resident experience, and stronger control of cyber risk.

Interoperability is the real strategic issue

When systems are connected in silos, data becomes fragmented and insight becomes difficult. A connected building strategy should focus on interoperability between HVAC, lighting, access, security, BMS and operational technology layers.

That does not require a single vendor or one platform to rule them all. It does require a clear architecture baseline, defined data standards and disciplined governance over change.

Cybersecurity and operational resilience must be designed in

Connected buildings introduce real cyber risk when asset owners overlook security controls in day-to-day operational technology decisions. Basic safeguards such as network segmentation, identity controls, monitored remote access and clear ownership of device configuration are essential.

For building owners, resilience ultimately depends on balancing user convenience with operational robustness.

Make the data useful

The value of connected building systems comes from actionable insight. Dashboards, alarms and sensor data only create value when they inform maintenance decisions, occupant experience and capital prioritisation.

That requires a disciplined operating model that ties information to accountability, ensuring owners can translate data into measurable service improvements.
